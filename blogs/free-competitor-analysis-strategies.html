How to Conduct a Competitor Analysis for Free

Karan Timba
February 14, 2026
A visual overview of free tools and strategies used to analyze competitors and grow a business.

Let’s be real for a second. When you start a business—whether it’s a gym, a digital marketing agency, or a dropshipping store—you want to know what the "big guys" are doing.

You want to know their keywords, their traffic sources, and their secret strategies.

But then you go to sign up for tools like Semrush or Ahrefs, and you see the price tag: $129/month (approx ₹10,000).

For a beginner or a small business owner, that is a lot of money just to "look" at data.

So, does that mean you have to fly blind? Absolutely not.

In fact, some of the best competitor analysis can be done for ₹0 if you know where to look and how to use free tools.

You don't need a fancy dashboard; you need a brain and a browser.

In this guide, I’m going to show you how to spy on your competitors, steal their best ideas, and find their weak spots—all without spending a single rupee.

Why Do You Even Need Competitor Analysis?

Think of business like a cricket match. You wouldn't walk onto the pitch without knowing who the bowler is, right?

You watch their previous matches. You see where they pitch the ball. You find their weak spots.

Competitor analysis in business is like cricket strategy—study opponents first to gain a winning advantage.

Competitor analysis is exactly that. It helps you:

  • Find "Content Gaps": What are they not writing about? (That’s your opportunity).
  • Steal Their Keywords: See what terms are bringing them traffic.
  • Understand Their Funnel: How do they turn a visitor into a customer?
  • Avoid Their Mistakes: If their site is slow or their checkout process is annoying, you can do it better.

Who Are Your Actual Competitors?

Most people get this wrong. They think their competitor is the biggest brand in the world.

If you open a coffee shop in your city, Starbucks is not your competitor. The cafe is down the street.

Using Google search and Maps results to identify real local competitors for effective competitor analysis.

How to Find Them:

  1. Open Google. (Make sure you are in Incognito Mode so your personal search history doesn't mess up the results).
  2. Type in your main service keywords:
    • “café near me”
    • “best coffee shop in [city]”
    • “iced coffee near me”
  3. Look at the top 3-5 results. Ignore the big directories like JustDial or Amazon. Look for actual businesses like yours.
Pro Tip: Look at the Google Maps Pack (the map section). These are your real local competitors.

Note down their:

  • Name
  • Review count (e.g., 45 reviews)
  • Star rating (e.g., 4.2 stars)
  • What people are complaining about in the bad reviews. (This is gold!) If people say, "WIFI is not working," you market your cafe as "cafe with free WIFI."

Keyword Spying (Without Paid Tools)

You don’t need a paid tool to find keywords. Google tells you exactly what people are searching for—you just have to listen.

1. The "Alphabet Soup" Method

Go to Google search and type your main keyword, but don't hit enter.

Type: "Digital marketing for..."

Google will suggest: "...small business," "...doctors," and "...real estate."

These suggestions are high-intent keywords. Google is showing them because thousands of people are actually searching for them.

Using Google Autosuggest to discover keyword opportunities and topics competitors are targeting.

2. "People Also Ask" (PAA)

Scroll down a bit on the search result page. You will see a box called "People also ask."

  • “How much does SEO cost in India?”
  • “Is social media better than SEO?”

Click on one question, and Google will load 2-3 more. Keep clicking. In 5 minutes, you will have a list of 20+ blog post ideas that your competitors are ranking for.

3. Google Keyword Planner (The "Hidden" Free Tool)

You know Google Ads? You can create a free account (you don't have to run an ad).

Inside, there is a tool called "Keyword Planner."

  • Put in your competitor’s URL.
  • Select "Entire Site."
  • Click "Get Results."

Boom. Google will show you a list of keywords that are relevant to that site. It won't be as detailed as Ahrefs, but it gives you volume and competition levels for free.

Analyzing Their Content Strategy

Now that we have keywords, let's look at their "Library." If their site is a library, we need to see what books they have on the shelves.

The site: Operator Hack

Go to Google and type: site:competitorwebsite.com

This will show you every single page Google has indexed from their site.

  • Do they have 50 pages or 500?
  • Are they writing blogs? How often?
  • Check their Titles. Are they catchy? "10 Tips for..." vs "Services Page."

The "Skyscraper" Technique

Find their best content. Let’s say they have a blog on "Top 10 SEO Tips." Read it.

  • Is it outdated? (Written in 2023?)
  • Is it hard to read? (Huge blocks of text?)
  • Is it missing images or videos?

Your Goal: Write on the same topic, but make it 10x better. Update it for 2026, add a video, add better examples, and format it with clear headings.

Technical Spying (Peeking Under the Hood)

You might think, "My competitor is ranking #1, their site must be perfect." Actually, mostly not.

Many top-ranking sites have terrible technical SEO. If you can fix these on your site, you can beat them.

Using PageSpeed Insights to evaluate competitor website speed and identify performance improvement opportunities.

1. Check Their Speed (Core Web Vitals)

Go to Google PageSpeed Insights (it’s free). Put in your competitor’s URL.

  • LCP (Largest Contentful Paint): Does it take 4 seconds to load? If yours loads in 2 seconds, Google will prefer you.
  • Mobile Friendliness: Check their site on your phone. Is the text too small? Are buttons hard to click?

2. Check Their "Robots"

Type competitorwebsite.com/robots.txt in your browser.

Sometimes you can see interesting things here. Are they hiding certain folders? It gives you a clue about their structure.

3. Check Their Sitemap

Type competitorwebsite.com/sitemap.xml.

This is a map of their entire website structure. You can see their categories, their landing pages, and exactly when they last updated a page. If their "lastmod" date is 2024, you know their content is stale.

The Social Media Stakeout

SEO is great, but social media tells you how they talk to customers.

Using Meta Ad Library to analyze competitor ads and discover winning marketing campaigns.

The "Meta Ad Library" (The Ultimate Spy Tool)

This is the best-kept secret in digital marketing.

  1. Go to "Facebook Ad Library" on Google.
  2. Search for your competitor’s brand name.
  3. If they are running ads, you can see them.

You can see:

  • What images/videos they are using.
  • What headlines they are using.
  • What offers they are pushing (e.g., "Free Trial" vs "50% Off").

Why is this useful? Because if they have been running an ad for 3 months, it means it’s making money. You don't have to guess what works; just look at their long-running ads and model your offer after them.

LinkedIn & Instagram

Go to their company page. Look at the "Posts" section.

  • Engagement: Do they get likes? Or is it a ghost town?
  • Content Type: Are they posting Reels? Carousels?
  • Comments: Read the comments! Are customers asking questions? Answer those questions in your own content.

Backlinks (The Hardest Part for Free)

Okay, checking backlinks for free is tough because this data costs money to crawl. However, there is a free trick to do it.

Using Ubersuggest to analyze competitor backlinks and discover link-building opportunities.

Ubersuggest (Free Version)

Neil Patel’s tool, Ubersuggest, allows you a few free searches a day. Put in your competitor’s domain and click "Backlinks." It will show you some of the sites linking to them.

Manual Search

Search for your competitor's brand name in quotes: "Competitor Name" -site:competitor.com

This tells Google: "Show me every page that mentions this brand, but exclude their own website."

You will see:

  • Guest posts they have written.
  • News articles about them.
  • Directories they are listed in.
Action Item: If they are listed in a "Top 10 Gyms in Mumbai" directory, you should contact that directory owner and ask to be added too.

Summary: Your Free Competitor Analysis Checklist

You don't need a budget. You need a process. Here is your checklist:

  • Identify Real Competitors: Use Google Search and Maps (Incognito).
  • Keyword Research: Use Google Autosuggest, PAA, and Keyword Planner.
  • Content Audit: Use site: operator to see their pages and find gaps.
  • Technical Check: Use PageSpeed Insights to find their speed weaknesses.
  • Ad Spying: Use Facebook Ad Library to see their paid strategy.
  • Backlink Check: Use manual search operators to find where they are mentioned.

Final Thoughts

Competitor analysis isn't about copying. If you just copy them, you will always be one step behind.

The goal is to Analyze, Adapt, and Improve. If they have a 2000-word guide on SEO, you write a 2500-word guide with a video.

If they have a boring Instagram, you make funny Reels.

If their site is slow, you make yours lightning fast.

Digital marketing is a race, but you don't have to be the fastest runner to win. You just have to know the shortcuts. And now, you do.

So, go open that Incognito tab and start scouting. Your competition is waiting.

Tools Used in This Guide

Here are the free and professional tools mentioned in this guide for competitor research and marketing analysis:

  • Semrush – Advanced SEO and competitor analysis platform (paid).
  • Ahrefs – Popular SEO and backlink research tool (paid).
  • Google Search – Find competitors and keyword opportunities.
  • Google Keyword Planner (inside Google Ads) – Free keyword data tool.
  • Google PageSpeed Insights – Website speed and performance checker.
  • Meta Ad Library – View competitor Facebook and Instagram ads.
  • LinkedIn – Analyze competitor company content and engagement.
  • Instagram – Study competitor social media strategies.
  • Ubersuggest by Neil Patel – Free limited competitor keyword and backlink analysis.
